FIrstly the mean of the numeric variable works because `random_sample`
is numeric. The mean of the logical variable also works. In R, logical
values are treated numerically when calculating the mean: TRUE is
treated as 1 and FALSE is treated as 0. Therefore, the mean of the
logical vector represents the proportion of observations that are TRUE,
which is same as the proportion of values in the random sample that are
greater than 0 in this case.

The character and factor variables do not produce meaningful means
because they are not numeric variables.

``` r
as.numeric(pull(problem2_df, logical_vector))

as.numeric(pull(problem2_df, character_vector))
```

    ## Warning: NAs introduced by coercion

``` r
as.numeric(pull(problem2_df, factor_vector))
```

Applying `as.numeric()` to the logical vector converts `TRUE` to 1 and
`FALSE` to 0. This explains why taking the mean of a logical variable
works.Applying it to the character vector produces `NA` values because
the character values such as `"a"` and `"b"` cannot be directly
interpreted as numbers. Applying it to the factor vector converts the
factor levels to their underlying integer codes. For example, the three
factor levels are internally represented by integers such as 1, 2, and
3. I think these results help explain why the mean works for numeric and
logical variables, but not for character or factor variables.
